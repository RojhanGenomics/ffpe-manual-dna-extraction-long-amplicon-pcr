# Portfolio Case Study

## Manual FFPE DNA Extraction for Long-Amplicon PCR

**Role:** Researcher / protocol optimization  
**Author:** Mahdieh Rojhannezhad, Ph.D.

### Problem
FFPE tissue can yield fragmented DNA, limiting recovery of longer PCR products. The practical goal was to improve access to longer amplifiable fragments using a manual workflow suitable for resource-constrained laboratories.

### Optimization
A mineral-oil deparaffinization step was added at the beginning of manual TRIzol-based extraction. Tissue was mechanically fragmented on a clean chilled surface with a scalpel rather than liquid nitrogen. PCR used a hot-start master mix and enhancer buffer.

### Result
Before/after gel evidence showed recovery of longer PCR bands after optimization. The researcher reports products reaching approximately 700–1000 bp in a subset of samples, with the improvement not occurring in every sample.

### Claim boundary
This is not a claim that mineral oil was newly invented or that the method universally outperforms kits. The contribution is practical optimization and demonstration of a resource-accessible workflow that improved long-amplicon PCR performance in part of the sample set.

### Skills demonstrated
FFPE molecular workflow optimization, manual DNA extraction, PCR troubleshooting, gel electrophoresis, experimental comparison, evidence calibration, and resource-aware laboratory method development.
