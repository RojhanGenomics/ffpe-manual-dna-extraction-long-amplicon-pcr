# Final Project Report

## Manual FFPE DNA Extraction for Long-Amplicon PCR

### Author
Mahdieh Rojhannezhad, Ph.D.  
ORCID: https://orcid.org/0009-0009-2427-718X

## 1. Objective
Optimize a manual DNA extraction approach for FFPE tissue with the practical objective of preserving longer amplifiable DNA fragments for PCR, especially in settings where commercial extraction kits and columns are less accessible.

## 2. Optimization strategy
The researcher added mineral-oil deparaffinization at the beginning of the workflow, before the manual TRIzol-based extraction. The tissue was mechanically fragmented on a clean chilled slide/foil with a scalpel rather than using liquid nitrogen.

The subsequent extraction used the established manual workflow, including TRIzol and Proteinase K, with the reported sodium-citrate/ethanol step. PCR was performed using a hot-start master mix and enhancer buffer.

## 3. Reported outcome
After optimization, longer PCR products were obtained in a subset of samples, with reported amplicon lengths reaching approximately 700–1000 bp. The improvement was not observed in every sample.

## 4. Claim calibration
The project does not claim to have invented mineral-oil deparaffinization. The defensible claim is narrower: using an accessible mineral-oil step within a manual FFPE DNA extraction workflow, together with the reported PCR system, was practically useful for recovering longer PCR-amplicon targets in a subset of samples where such longer products had not previously been obtained.

This is a feasibility/optimization observation, not a controlled demonstration of superiority over commercial kits or other extraction methods.

## 5. Evidence and limitations
The available gel images provide visual evidence of the before/after optimization comparison. The ladder is 100 bp. Quantitative DNA integrity metrics, complete sample-level success tables, independent replicate statistics, and a controlled comparison against commercial kits are not available in the surviving record.

## 6. Conclusion
The optimized manual workflow provided a practical route to longer PCR-amplifiable DNA from FFPE material under the reported laboratory constraints, with longer amplicons obtained in approximately half of the tested samples according to the researcher's account. The result should be presented as an experimentally useful optimization rather than a universally superior extraction method.
